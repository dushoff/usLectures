SEC Approach and assumptions

----------------------------------------------------------------------

What is a linear model?

	A \emph{fit} to a \textbf{response variable} based on assumptions about its relationship to one or more \textbf{predictor variables}

	\emph{Inferences} about parameters based on assumptions about the \emph{conditional} distribution of the response variable

		ANS How it is distributed for given values of the predictor variables

	A linear model gives the response variable as a linear combination of the \emph{parameters} it is estimating

		ANS It is not necessarily linear with respect to predictor variables!

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Power

	Linear models are very efficient

		They find the best “least-squares” fit with simple linear algebra

	They can be very powerful at detecting effects

		They can also be over-sensitive to outliers

		COMMENT Spiderman: with great power comes great responsibility

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Assumptions

	Linear-model \emph{fits} are basically always correct, but can be hard to interpret

	Linear-model \emph{inferences} (parameter estimates P-values, CIs) depend on assumptions about \emph{residuals}

	A residual is $y-\hat y$: the difference between an observed value $y$ and a model prediction $\hat y$

	lm inference assumes that residuals are independent and normally distributed, with constant variance

		The mean of the residuals is always 0

		ANS Otherwise the machine would find a better linear model

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Linear model?

$y$ is response, $x$ is predictor

	$y = a + b x$

	$y = a + b x + c x^2 + d x^3$

	$y = \frac{a}{b+x}$

	$y = a x^b$

	$\log(y) = a + b \log(x)$

	ANS 1, 2 and 5 are lms

	ANS 4 and 5 are the same equation, but imply a different \emph{distribution} for $y$

Binary predictor

	Two-group test

		Predictor with two values (could be continuous or categorical)

		Can men hold their breath longer than women?

		Are mice more active at 10 degrees or 20 degrees C?

	Assumptions

		ANS Each group is normally distributed

		ANS Same variance 

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Testing assumptions

	ANS Residuals should look approximately normal

	ANS and approximately the same across the two groups

	Same answers as above

		ANS Permutation test

		ANS Different scale

		ANS Medians

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Parameterizing

	Different two-group models have the same fit

		predicted mean for each individual

	but different parameterizations

		mean and difference

		baseline and difference

		intercept and slope

	lm does not work with too many parameters

		ANS You would need to choose two in this case

		ANS Because we're interested in a mean for each group

Paired t-test

	Example: do people have more joint erosion on their dominant hand?

		ANS Can't treat my left hand and right hand as independent!

	How to do it?

		ANS The paired t-test is equivalent to a one-sample test on the \emph{differences}!

		ANS Procedure is the same, but now we're particularly interested in the \emph{sign} of the estimate

Categorical predictor

	Anova test

	We \emph{add} $n-1$ new parameters for a variable with $n$ categories

		We also add $n-1$ “lm variables” to the calculation

		These are often called “dummy variables” 

		They code which category each observation is in, using 0s and 1s
